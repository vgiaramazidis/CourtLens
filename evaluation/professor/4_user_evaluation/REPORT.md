# CourtLens user evaluation

Ten participants completed nine application tasks independently, according to the study owner's confirmation. The task sheet records 90 successes out of 90 participant-task outcomes. Five post-task questions produced 50 ratings. Qualitative feedback is available for all ten participants.

## Sources and study design

The English-only [Excel workbook](results/CourtLens_User_Evaluation.xlsx) is the data source for this analysis and both CourtLens reports. It contains Summary, Ratings, Tasks, Comments and Themes sheets.

- Tasks: ten participant rows, nine binary task columns and English task definitions.
- Ratings: five scores per participant, participant means, normalized means, descriptive statistics, frequency counts and English question wording.
- Comments: three comment fields per participant.
- Themes: twelve reported theme frequencies.

T1–T9 follow the ordered task definitions in the workbook. No baseline-condition results were supplied, so no comparison with EuroLeague plus YouTube is reported.

The scale anchors are 1 = Not at all / Very poor and 5 = Very much / Excellent. All participant identifiers remain pseudonymous. Theme counts reproduce the supplied analysis. No independent coding or inter-coder agreement is claimed.

## Qualitative findings

The supplied theme analysis reports interactive games and information/tool variety at 5/10 each, synchronized video at 4/10, and the combined court/AI theme at 2/10. Reported difficulties include interface visibility or guidance at 5/10, court navigation at 3/10, quiz difficulty progression at 3/10, and AI speed/freezing at 1/10.

Reported suggestions include improved AI at 5/10, expanded games/filters/competitions at 3/10, new or richer game modes at 3/10, and remembering Video Analysis context at 1/10. Categories overlap and must not be summed into a participant total. The participant-to-theme coding matrix was not supplied.

Users 2, 5 and 6 describe court-navigation difficulties, with User 6 mentioning mobile orientation and precise shot selection. Users 3 and 10 emphasize synchronized video access. Users 5, 8 and 10 request better quiz difficulty progression. User 1 reports slow or frozen AI Search. These comments contextualize the spread in Q2 and Q3 despite universal task completion.

## Interpretation and limitations

The results support independent completion of the recorded tasks and a positive reported experience in this sample. They also support evaluating a steadier court view, clearer controls and guidance, more reliable AI Search and better quiz progression. These are proposed follow-ups, not changes made to the application during report preparation.

Session dates, recruitment, participant demographics, device allocation, software version, completion times, attempts and error logs were not supplied. No baseline comparison, inferential statistical test, accessibility certification, learning gain or direct user-study OCR error measurement is claimed.
